# Meat-Me-Halfway
I utilized a dumbbell chart to get a side-by-side look at how my KBBQ favorites stack up against my friend's ratings from our Iron Age birthday dinner: showing where our tastes align and where they definitely don’t.

# Goals 
A fun casual head-to-head on meat ratings between friends. Both of us are data majors, so it seemed like an engaging way to gauge taste preferences and make a fun data viz!

# Analysis Questions and Findings 
- Did our ratings align on any meat? 
    - **Nope! We did not state the same rating for any meat!** 
- What meat were you most split on? 
    - **Soy Pork Steak! I thought it was plain, but he enjoyed it, perhaps because it was a good vessel to carry the salt & pepper sesame oil he was dipping it in!**
 
# Data Collection and Data Opportunities 
To collect this data, we said our rating out loud on a count to 3. This prevented any bias introduced by one of us sharing our rating early. Biggest data opportunity would to have been eating more kinds of meat or recording meat flavor profiles to see if there were any patterns. Keeping it simple made sure the meal was still leisurely and less of a discussion on data collection practices.

<img width="618" height="567" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c8b92041-3b80-4be1-b224-a75d15924ddd" />
<br>
Something I am constantly working on is not overthinking and trying to bend data in ways it’s not built for. For instance, even in my data opportunities I discussed adding flavor notes to the dataset, but doing so would exponentially change this data viz and honestly with little benefit. So, I kept this to the core measure of his opinion versus mine. I had seen these kinds of graphs before and enjoyed the comparison emphasis. It turns out that these graphs are called dumbbell charts! <br>
<br>
I included the hands because I also think it emphasizes one versus another. The idea was to personalize it even further by taking pictures of our hands holding the chopsticks... but that didn’t go well (angles etc etc).  The chopsticks because that's the common tool used when eating at a KBBQ (don’t tell anyone I asked for a fork though)!
